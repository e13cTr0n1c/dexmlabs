The physics of getting to orbit

Orbit isn't about height, it's about speed. At 150 km you need about 7.8 km/s sideways so that you fall around the Earth instead of into it. Add what you lose climbing against gravity and pushing through the air and a real rocket needs roughly 9.3 to 10 km/s of delta v. Launching east gets you a free 465 m/s from the Earth's spin at the equator, which the game includes.

The rocket equation decides how much fuel that takes. Delta v is the exhaust speed times the log of full mass over empty mass. Exhaust speed is specific impulse times 9.81. A kerosene engine like this one gets about 282 seconds at sea level and 311 in vacuum, a hydrogen engine about 450, but with far less thrust here. Every tonne you add to the top stage costs fuel in both stages, which is why staging and a light upper stage matter so much.

The game flies a simple gravity turn: the rocket tilts gradually as it climbs, flying flat by the height you choose, then the second stage steers to stop climbing. The air thins by about a factor of e every 8.5 km. Turn too early and you fight the thick air, too late and you burn fuel just going up.

What's simplified

It's two dimensional, the steering is automatic, and tank masses are rounded. There's no wind, no engine out and no landing the booster. A launch counts as orbit when the lowest point of its path is above 150 km. The engine figures are close to real kerosene and hydrogen engines but aren't any one engine's spec sheet.

Questions

How fast do you need to go to reach orbit?
About 7.8 km/s sideways at 150 to 200 km up. Getting there takes roughly 9.3 to 10 km/s of delta v once you add the losses to gravity and air.
Why do rockets have stages?
Dropping empty tanks and engines means the rest of the fuel only has to push what's left. The rocket equation makes that much more efficient than one big stage.
What is the rocket equation?
Delta v equals the engine's exhaust speed times the natural log of the starting mass over the final mass. Tsiolkovsky published it in 1903.
What is a gravity turn?
The rocket goes up first to get out of the thick air, then tilts gradually so gravity bends its path towards horizontal. Turning too early wastes speed fighting the air, too late wastes it fighting gravity.
Is this game realistic?
The physics is real: gravity, a thinning atmosphere, Earth's rotation and the rocket equation. The engines are modelled on real kerosene and hydrogen engines but simplified, and the steering is a simple automatic programme.
