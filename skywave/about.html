FROM THE WORKBENCH / ABOUT

A SMALL GAME.
A BIG WORLD.

Skywave is a free daily puzzle about making the right contact at the right time. No account. No cookies. Just you, a radio and the ionosphere.

Skywave is a game. Its propagation model is simplified and is not a prediction tool. For real conditions use VOACAP or live propagation data.

Fictional stations. Familiar physics.

Callsigns are generated from real ITU prefix families and random suffixes. All stations are fictional; any match with a real station is a coincidence. The model is a deliberately simplified game system, not a forecast, a real DX cluster or a transmitter.

On your device, not our server

Daily conditions and targets are calculated from the UTC date. Progress, preferences, best scores and streaks use localStorage in this browser only; practice results do not affect the daily streak. The site has no backend, analytics, accounts, audio recordings, cookies or advertising. See cookies and storage for how to clear it.

Only the pinned three.js library and its matching addons are loaded from jsDelivr. Earth images and game code load from the same static site. Gear and support links leave the site only when clicked.

Assets and acknowledgements

NASA Blue Marble supplies the day map. The bundled derivative comes from the Basemap data package and has been resized and compressed. NASA imagery is used under its media guidelines; NASA does not endorse this game.

NIGHT MAP: the city lights on the night side are an illustrative texture made for this game, not NASA imagery. For the real thing see NASA Black Marble.

three.js r185 / 0.185.0 and its addons are used under the MIT licence. The coarse land mask is derived from public-domain Natural Earth data. Prefix families follow the ITU call-sign series.

All audio is generated with WebAudio. Interface artwork and the fallback lights are original; no audio files or web fonts are used. Full credits and source URLs are included with the source.

Under the hood

Plain JavaScript, HTML and CSS. The propagation model is separate from the renderer, so its tests run without three.js. When WebGL2 or the CDN is unavailable, a labelled Canvas fallback keeps the radio desk playable.
