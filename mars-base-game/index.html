    Today's job

    Each build uses 2 spare parts. Power runs the habitat first, then oxygen, water and greenhouses. Lose habitat power, or run out of anything, and the crew has to leave.

    The science of staying alive on Mars

    A person needs about 0.84 kg of oxygen a day, so a crew of four gets through about 3.4 kg. The game also counts 12 kg of water a day for the crew after recycling, and 6 kg of food. The International Space Station recovers most of its water, but nothing is perfect, so a Mars base would need to dig ice as well.

    Oxygen can be made from the air. Mars's thin atmosphere is mostly carbon dioxide, and NASA's MOXIE experiment on Perseverance split it into oxygen and carbon monoxide 16 times. A full size plant would take something like 10 kWh for each kilogram, which is the rate used here. Sunlight on Mars is about 43% as strong as on Earth, and dust in the air dims it further. The game uses the dust's optical depth, tau: solar output falls by a factor of e for each extra unit of tau. A clear sol is around 0.5. A big storm can pass 3, and in 2018 one did, and the Opportunity rover never woke up again.

    What's simplified

    The machines here are rounded and much smaller than any real plan, and a real base would carry backups for everything. Mission sites are real places scientists have studied, but the forecasts are made up for the game.

    Questions

    How much oxygen does an astronaut need a day?
    About 0.84 kg. NASA's planning figures for a crew of four come to about 3.4 kg a day.
    Can you make oxygen on Mars?
    Yes. NASA's MOXIE experiment on the Perseverance rover made oxygen from the carbon dioxide in the Martian air 16 times between 2021 and 2023, about 122 grams in total.
    Is there water on Mars?
    Yes, as ice. There is ice in the polar caps and buried under the surface at middle latitudes, which is why some proposed landing sites are where it is.
    How bad are Martian dust storms?
    Bad enough to end missions. A planet wide storm in 2018 cut the light reaching the Opportunity rover's solar panels so much that it never woke up again.
    Is this game realistic?
    The crew needs and the dust effect are real figures. The machines are simplified, and real bases would carry far more spares and backups than this one.
