MMX mission explained: interactive simulation

JAXA's Martian Moons eXploration spacecraft left Tanegashima on an H3 rocket on 20 October 2026. It is going to Phobos, the larger moon of Mars, to pick up at least 10 g of its surface and bring it home in 2031. I built this replay so I could see the whole trip at once.     What is a quasi satellite orbit?

    Phobos is about 26 km across and its gravity is far too weak to hold a spacecraft in an ordinary orbit around it. So MMX orbits Mars instead, on a path that keeps it close to Phobos. Seen from Phobos, MMX seems to loop round the moon in an oval twice as tall as it is wide. That is the small inset in the close view.

    What is to scale

    Earth and Mars come from JPL's approximate orbital elements, so where they sit on any date is real. Mars, Phobos and Deimos are drawn to scale in the close view, but the moons move faster than life so you can see them. The transfer paths, the capture orbit, the ascent track and the landing are my drawings of the idea, not flight data.
