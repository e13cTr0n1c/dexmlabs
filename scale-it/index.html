How to play

There are five rounds. One asks which of two is larger. One asks you to put three in order, smallest to largest. The others ask for a number on a slider: a length, a speed, a weight, or how many of a smaller thing fit inside a larger one.

Picking the larger one scores 1,000 or nothing. The right order scores 1,000, one swapped pair scores 600, and a full mix up scores 200. On a slider, within 2 percent is 1,000, and the score falls evenly to nothing when you are 50 percent out. A day is out of 5,000.

The picture after each answer is the point. The shapes are mine, drawn to the real measurement, with a person at 1.75 m so you can feel the size. On a phone, tap the cards or drag the slider. On a keyboard, 1 and 2 pick a pair, and 1, 2 and 3 place an order.

Questions
Is it the same puzzle for everyone?
Yes. The five rounds change at midnight in the UK, and everyone playing that day gets the same five.
How is a round scored?
Picking the larger one is 1,000 or nothing. Putting three in the right order is 1,000, one swap is 600, and a full mix up is 200. A slider is 1,000 within 2 percent and falls to nothing at 50 percent out.
Where do the numbers come from?
Each figure is copied from a public page, and that page is linked on the reveal. If I could not find a source, the thing is not in the game.
Where is my streak kept?
In this browser only. There is no account. Clear the site data and the streak goes with it.

About

I'm Arthur. I like a number I can stand next to, because the picture in my head is usually wrong. The person in the drawing is 1.75 m tall, just a scale, not anyone in particular. I am not a pilot and this is not a training aid. If a figure looks wrong, email me and I will check the source.
