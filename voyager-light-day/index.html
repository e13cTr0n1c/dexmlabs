Voyager 1

Voyager Light-Day

A countdown to one light day from Earth.

On Wednesday 18 November 2026, at 10:16:07 GMT, NASA says Voyager 1 will be one light day from Earth. That is 5:16:07 in the morning Eastern Standard Time, and 2:16:07 in the morning Pacific Standard Time. I count down to that instant, and I keep a rough distance beside it.

Distance from Earth

This is a straight line, not a live feed from the Deep Space Network. At the mark I set the distance to the length of one light day that NASA publishes, 25,902,068,356 kilometres (16,094,799,096 miles). I then add 25.407 kilometres a second. That is the rate at which the gap from Earth was growing in the JPL Horizons table around that moment, rounded to three decimal places.

Earth moves, so the real gap speeds up and slows down through the year, and for part of the year it shrinks. Close to the date this line is near the truth. Further away it is only a sketch. NASA also says Voyager 1 is cruising at nearly 38,000 miles an hour (61,000 kilometres an hour). That is how fast it moves through space, past the Sun. It is not the rate I use here.

One astronomical unit is 149,597,870.7 kilometres. Light in this sum travels at 299,792.458 kilometres a second. Both figures are the ones Horizons prints. I treat NASA's published distance as exactly 24 hours of light. Their kilometre figure is about 15 km short of a strict 24 hours at that speed, which is rounding, so the clock reads 24:00:00.000 at the mark and runs from there. A second of light time is about 300,000 km, so that clock gains one second only every few hours. The kilometres tick every second.

Flash VGR

NASA invites people to take part after sunset on Tuesday 17 November 2026, flashing VGR, Voyager's call sign, in Morse. Phones, torches and even whole buildings, a wave of light going round the Earth with the night. NASA's page for it is go.nasa.gov/shinebright. The button plays the same pattern on this screen, with a beep for each mark. It stays on your device. It is not a radio transmission.

V is dot dot dot dash. G is dash dash dot. R is dot dash dot. A dot is 120 milliseconds. A dash is three dots. The gap inside a letter is one dot. The gap between letters is three dots. If you tick repeat, I wait seven dots before I start again, the usual gap between words.

Flashing lights

This fills the whole screen with white, on and off, in time with the beeps. Flashing lights can make some people unwell, and they can trigger seizures. If that is you, leave it off. When your browser is set to reduce motion, I ask before the screen flashes.
