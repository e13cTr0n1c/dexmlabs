Why I built it

Lorenz is a free daily puzzle about the German teleprinter cipher that Bletchley Park called Tunny. I'm Arthur, and I made it.

This is a game. The cam patterns and messages are made up for it, and nothing here is a real intercept.

Me and radio

I'm into planes and I live near RAF Coningsby. Radio came in through the back door: I set up an SDR on a Raspberry Pi to pick up ADS-B from aircraft. Now I'm studying for my Foundation licence.

Why Tunny

Before this I made Skywave, a game about how HF radio signals get round the world. Tunny is the same world from another angle. The most important German messages of the war went out as enciphered teleprinter signals, by radio, and British stations sat and listened to them. Then a handful of people at Bletchley Park worked out the machine making them without ever seeing it... I wanted to understand how, so I built one I could play with.

The machine logic follows the wartime General Report on Tunny as closely as I could manage. The wheels, cam counts, motor logic, tape and alphabet are real. The cam patterns and messages are mine. The guide says exactly which is which, with sources.

Print your own

A simplified printable model is on the way. The Chi only mode in practice works the same way as that model.

What's stored, and where

Everything stays in your browser. Each day's intercept is worked out from the UTC date. Your progress, settings, best score and streak are kept in this browser's localStorage. Practice rounds don't touch your streak. There's no backend, no accounts, no cookies and no advertising. I count visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which sets no cookies and only gives me totals. See cookies and storage for how to clear it all.

The only things loaded from elsewhere are the pinned three.js library (from jsDelivr), for the 3D view, and Cloudflare's small analytics script. If three.js can't load, a flat view takes over and the game still works.
